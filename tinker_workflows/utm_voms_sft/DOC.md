### Understanding the Representations Distilled into Universally Pretrained Language Models

Two recent papers have been published that explore how to improve the generalization of pretrained language models. The first was "Learning Universal Predictors", and another one, published almost at the same time, was "Universal pretraining via iterated random computation". The circuits representing the processes by which a Transformer language model is able to generalize better after being pretrained with random computation are poorly understood. Further, choices of metrics have a significant impact on the algorithmic complexity of the resulting model, helping us select the kinds of computations that could also distill strong Solomonoff-like inductive biases in a Looped Transformer (LTF). The metric in question, epiplexity (From Entropy to Epiplexity: Rethinking Information for Computationally Bounded Intelligence) is applied in the paper to select classes of software programs whose computations are predicted by LTF by several steps i.e for a timestep k, we predict (16 + k) to (64 + k) steps ahead in a curriculum. The choice of program to predict has consequences, and so cellular automata have been empirically determined to contain high quality data when measured via epiplexity. In order to understand how all of this helps with generalization, we aim to visualize how internal representations differ between the various training regimes that follow each step of the training processes involved in these mechanisms.
